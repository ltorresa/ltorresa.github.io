Lorenzo Torresani is a Professor and President Joseph E. Aoun Chair at Northeastern University, Khoury College of Computer Sciences, and a founding lead of Northeastern's Physical AI Research (PAIR) Center, where he leads the FarSight Lab. He is the author of the first methods for 3D reconstruction of non-rigid objects and influential architectures for image and video analysis (e.g., Classemes, C3D, TimeSformer). His current research focuses on perception, anticipation, and planning in dynamic worlds, including egocentric video understanding for wearable assistants, large-scale learning from noisy video corpora, and strategic reasoning over multi-agent scenes such as team sports. He received his Ph.D. in Computer Science from Stanford University in 2005. In the past, he held a tenured Professor position at Dartmouth College and was a Research Director at Meta, Fundamental AI Research (FAIR), where he led a team in multimodal video understanding. He also worked at Microsoft Research, Like.com, and Digital Persona. He is the recipient of multiple awards, including a CVPR Best Student Paper prize, a National Science Foundation CAREER Award, a Google Faculty Research Award, three Facebook Faculty Awards, and a Fulbright U.S. Scholar Award.
